# Phone and walk-in scripts

## The after-hours test (do this before you contact anyone)
1. Call the company between **6 PM and 9 PM** on a weekday, or on a Saturday.
2. Write down in your lead tracker what happened: *answered by a person / answering service / voicemail / no answer*, plus the time.
3. Hang up politely if a person answers ("Sorry, wrong number" is fine), or leave no message.
4. A company that went to voicemail is your best prospect. They have the exact problem you solve.

## Cold call (daytime, 30–60 seconds)
Best times: **10–11:30 AM or 1:30–3 PM**, when owners are between jobs.

> "Hi, is this [owner name]? Hi [Name], this is [Your name], I'm local here in [city]. I'll be quick: I work with HVAC companies on missed calls. I actually called your line last [Tuesday] around [7:40] and got voicemail. Do you know roughly how many calls you miss after hours in a busy week?"

*(Listen. Let them talk.)*

> "That's pretty common. I built a system that texts those callers back instantly and can book emergencies straight onto your on-call tech's schedule. I'm doing a free 30-day pilot with a few local shops. Could I show you in 15 minutes, on Zoom or at your shop?"

**If they say "send me something":** "Sure. What's the best email? I'll send a 2-minute video and a one-pager." Then send email #1 within the hour.

## Walk-in visit (shop or supply house)
- **When:** 7–9 AM at supply houses (Johnstone Supply, Ferguson and similar), when contractors are picking up parts. At a shop, go mid-morning.
- **Bring:** printed one-pagers and your laptop or tablet with the demo already open.
- **Dress:** clean and simple. Smiling and friendly beats "salesy".

> "Hi, I'm [Name]. I'm working with local HVAC companies on catching after-hours calls and following up on estimates. Do you have 2 minutes? I can show you on my laptop."

Show the emergency call simulator. Leave the one-pager. Ask for the owner's name and email.

**Supply-house tip:** ask the counter staff which contractors are growing or complaining about being too busy. They know everyone. Ask permission before leaving flyers.

## Common objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We already have an answering service." | "Great, you care about calls. Does your service book the job onto your calendar and follow up on estimates? This does both, usually for less than a full answering service." |
| "Customers hate robots." | "Fair. That's why it's upfront, it hands off to a person the moment someone asks, and you can start with just text-back and follow-ups. No AI voice at all." |
| "I don't have time to set it up." | "I do all of it. You'd spend about 30 minutes with me total." |
| "How much is it?" | "The pilot is free for 30 days. After that it's $[X]/month, and you only continue if it pays for itself. One recovered repair usually covers a month." |
| "Send me info." | "Happy to. What's the one thing you'd want it to fix: nights, weekends, or estimates?" (Then tailor the email.) |
| "Not now, it's slow season." | "Makes sense. Slow season is actually the easiest time to set it up before the rush. Can I check back in [month]?" (Note it in your tracker.) |
| "What if it messes up with a customer?" | "Every message template is approved by you before it goes live, you can see every conversation, and you can take over any of them with one click." |
