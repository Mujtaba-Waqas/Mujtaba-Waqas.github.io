# Email templates

**Before you send:**
- Replace everything in [brackets].
- Keep emails short: people read them on their phones between jobs.
- Send from a professional address, ideally on your own domain.
- Send 10–20 emails a day, written one at a time. Don't blast hundreds.
- Every email must include your name, a postal address, and an opt-out line (CAN-SPAM).

---

## 1. Cold email: the after-hours test (strongest)
Use it when you called them after hours and got voicemail or no answer.

**Subject:** Called [Company] at [7:40] last night

> Hi [First name],
>
> I called [Company] last night at [7:40 PM] and got voicemail. That's normal after hours, but a homeowner with a broken AC usually just calls the next company on Google.
>
> I built a tool for [Utah] HVAC companies that answers those calls, books emergencies with your on-call tech, and texts back every missed call within seconds.
>
> Here's a 2-minute video of it handling a 9 PM "no AC" call: [Loom link]
>
> I'm offering a free 30-day pilot to a few local companies, and I do all the setup. Worth a quick look?
>
> [Your name]
> [Phone] · [Website/demo link]
> [Postal address]
> *Not interested? Just reply "no" and I won't email again.*

## 2. Cold email: estimate follow-up angle
Use it for companies that do a lot of installs and replacements.

**Subject:** The estimates that go quiet

> Hi [First name],
>
> Quick question: how many of [Company]'s install estimates last month never got a yes or no?
>
> CallFlow follows up on every open estimate automatically (day 1, 3 and 7) with friendly texts, and stops the moment the customer answers or asks for a person. Winning back even one $7,000 replacement pays for years of it.
>
> 2-minute demo: [Loom link]
>
> Open to a free 30-day pilot?
>
> [Signature + opt-out line]

## 3. Follow-up #1 (3–4 days later, reply in the same thread)

> Hi [First name], just floating this back up. Happy to show you in 15 minutes on Zoom, or I can stop by the shop. Does [Tuesday or Thursday morning] work?
>
> [Your name]

## 4. Follow-up #2 (about a week later, the last one)

> Hi [First name], I'll close the loop after this one. If after-hours calls or unanswered estimates ever become a headache, the free pilot offer stands. Here's a one-page overview: [one-pager PDF link]
>
> Thanks for your time,
> [Your name]

## 6. Referral ask (after a successful pilot)

> Hi [First name], really glad the pilot recovered [X jobs / $Y]. Do you know one or two other owners (not direct competitors) who'd want the same? A quick intro by email would mean a lot. I'll take great care of them.

## Finding the right email address
- Company website: Contact / About pages, often `owner@` or `info@`.
- Google Business Profile: the website and phone number.
- Facebook page: the "About" section.
- If there's only `info@`, use it and address the owner by name (search "[Company] owner" on Google or LinkedIn).
